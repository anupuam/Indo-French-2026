## 8. Value Creation — Growth and Employment (max 100 words)

This project creates value through three channels. First, it trains highly skilled researchers — at least four doctoral students and multiple postdoctoral fellows — in the globally scarce intersection of evolutionary computation, wearable robotics, and clinical rehabilitation engineering, directly expanding the qualified workforce in both countries. Second, the open-source algorithm libraries and public benchmark datasets lower barriers to entry for assistive robotics start-ups and SMEs, catalysing new ventures. Third, the technology transfer white paper and industrial partner engagement (Wipro GE Healthcare, Wandercraft SAS, Parker Hannifin) target licensing pathways that connect research outputs to manufacturing and clinical deployment, stimulating job creation in the assistive technology sector in India and France.

## 9. Expected Results of This Cooperation (up to 500 words)

The cooperation is expected to produce a comprehensive set of scientific, technological, and translational outputs that span publications, datasets, software, physical demonstrators, patents, and capacity-building activities.

**Joint Publications.** The project targets a minimum of 8 peer-reviewed journal publications in leading venues including IEEE Transactions on Neural Systems and Rehabilitation Engineering, Journal of NeuroEngineering and Rehabilitation, Robotics and Autonomous Systems, and IEEE Transactions on Evolutionary Computation. Additionally, at least 12 conference papers will be submitted to premier international conferences (ICRA, IROS, EMBC, BioRob, GECCO). All publications will carry joint Indo-French authorship, reflecting the genuinely collaborative nature of the research across every work package.

**Open-Access Datasets.** The Indo-French Gait Variability Dataset (IF-GVD) will comprise wearable IMU and plantar pressure recordings from a minimum of 60 participants (30 healthy adults, 15 elderly, 15 post-stroke patients) across both Indian and French sites, recorded under multiple locomotion conditions. This cross-continental, multi-pathology dataset — released publicly on Zenodo/PhysioNet — will be a first-of-its-kind resource for the international assistive robotics community. A complementary Exoskeleton Control Benchmark Dataset will provide joint kinematics, kinetics, and control signals from hardware experiments.

**Open-Source Software Libraries.** Three software deliverables will be released under permissive open-source licences (Apache 2.0): (i) a Fuzzy-CNN-GRU library with evolutionary Neural Architecture Search support for biomechanical time-series classification (Python/PyTorch); (ii) an Optimal Transport domain adaptation toolkit for gait trajectory personalization (Python, integrated with the POT library); and (iii) a σ-AADRC controller firmware with embedded Differential Evolution online tuning (C++/ROS2). These libraries provide directly usable components for both academic researchers and industrial developers of wearable robotic systems.

**Physical Demonstrators.** The project will deliver an instrumented lower-limb exoskeleton prototype (hip-knee-ankle, bilateral) with the complete Multi-Level Optimization stack running in real time, a wearable sensing suit with real-time data streaming, and a tablet-based clinician interface application for configuring assistance profiles.

**Patents.** Two patent applications — one filed in India and one in France — will protect commercially valuable innovations arising from the MLO framework, particularly the evolutionary NAS-optimised gait classification architecture and the σ-AADRC control design with metaheuristic online tuning. These patents are expected to have significant commercial value given that the global assistive robotics market, valued at approximately USD 2.8 billion in 2023, is projected to reach USD 9.6 billion by 2030 (CAGR 22%). The patent portfolio, combined with the technology transfer white paper targeting industrial partners (Wipro GE Healthcare, Wandercraft SAS, Parker Hannifin), establishes a concrete pathway from research prototype to licensed product. Letters of intent for collaborative product development will be sought in Year 3.

**Capacity Building.** The joint Indo-French summer school on "Optimization and AI for Assistive Robotics" and the structured researcher exchange programme (minimum four visits per year in each direction) will build a lasting bilateral research network and produce researchers with the rare combination of skills in evolutionary computation and experimental wearable robotics.

## 10. Details of How Intellectual Property Rights Will Be Shared (up to 100 words)

Intellectual property generated jointly will be co-owned by both institutions (Dr B.R. Ambedkar NIT Jalandhar and Université Paris-Est Créteil / Université Gustave Eiffel) in proportion to each team's inventive contribution, as determined by mutual agreement at the time of each invention disclosure. Patent filing and maintenance costs will be shared equally unless otherwise agreed. Each partner retains the right to use jointly owned IP for non-commercial academic research without restriction. Commercial licensing of joint IP requires written consent from both parties, with licensing revenues shared in proportion to ownership. A formal IP agreement will be executed before project commencement.
